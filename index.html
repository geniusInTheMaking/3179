Australia, Between the Pages

Exploring who reads, what Australians borrow, and how books connect communities.

Introduction

Every Page Has a Story. Some people escape into fantasy while others get lost in a mystery. From the books stacked on our bedside tables to the stories borrowed from local libraries, reading finds its way into our lives in all kinds of ways.

But what do Australians reach for when they pick up a book? Are reading habits changing with each generation, and do the places we live shape the stories we discover?

Australia, Between the Pages explores the diverse reading habits of Australians, examining how reading participation varies across generations and communities. Through national reading trends and public library data, the visualisation uncovers where library engagement is strongest, what types of books Australians read and borrow, and how their reading preferences differ.

02 - The People Behind the Pages

Reading habits extend beyond individual preferences. Across Australia, public libraries connect communities with books, resources and shared spaces. Yet the ways people engage with these services differ from place to place.

From library membership and borrowing activity to physical visits and digital collections, this chapter explores how public library engagement varies across Australian communities.

Library membership provides a different measure of engagement, showing what share of the population is registered with public library services across Australia. Comparing three of Australia's most populous states reveals a striking similarity in membership rates, despite their different population sizes.

New South Wales has the highest membership rate at 34.7%, leading Queensland by 3.6 percentage points. Victoria and Queensland differ by less than 0.5 points. However, around two-thirds of residents are not registered members. This does not necessarily mean low engagement, as people can share memberships or use library spaces and programs without registering.

Where Is Library Borrowing Strongest in NSW?

Public library borrowing varies considerably across New South Wales, influenced by population distribution, library accessibility and local reading demand. Sydney's metropolitan councils account for some of the largest borrowing volumes. T heir larger populations and potentially more extensive library networks may contribute to higher total circulation.

However, high borrowing volumes do not necessarily indicate stronger borrowing habits. Loans per resident account for population differences, revealing how intensively libraries are used relative to the communities they serve.

Differences in borrowing intensity may reflect library accessibility, collection availability and local demand. Areas with fewer total loans can still demonstrate substantial borrowing activity relative to their population.

Where Are NSW Libraries Most Visited?

Library engagement extends beyond borrowing books, with physical visits reflecting how communities use libraries for reading, studying and activities. Visits are concentrated around Sydney and coastal NSW, possibly due to higher population densities and better access to library branches.

Inland councils generally record fewer visits, potentially due to dispersed populations and longer travel distances. Beyond Physical Books: How Digital Is Library Borrowing in Queensland?

Digital collections allow readers to borrow books and other materials without visiting a physical library. However, the share of borrowing conducted digitally varies across Queensland councils.

These differences may reflect access to physical library branches, internet connectivity and local borrowing preferences. For geographically dispersed communities, digital collections may help overcome long travel distances.

However, a higher digital borrowing share does not necessarily indicate greater overall library use. Councils with fewer physical loans may still record a relatively high digital percentage.

03 - From Communities to Collections

Libraries connect Australians with books, but what readers choose from those shelves tells another story. From novels and non-fiction to graphic novels and poetry, reading preferences reflect different interests, experiences and audiences.

This chapter begins with the types of literature Australians enjoy, before looking more closely at borrowing patterns in public libraries. By exploring genres, languages and changes over time, we uncover how the books people choose contribute to Australia's diverse reading landscape.

What Types of Literature Do Australians Read?

Novels and short stories are the most widely read category, suggesting that storytelling, entertainment and escapism remain important motivations for reading among Australians.

However, instructional and creative non-fiction also attract substantial readership. This may reflect an interest in practical knowledge, personal development and real-world experiences, showing that reading serves purposes beyond entertainment.

Graphic novels, comics and poetry attract smaller shares of respondents, potentially reflecting more specialised interests rather than a lack of cultural significance.

Do Different Audiences Borrow Different Books?

Reading preferences can vary across generations, reflecting different interests, experiences and stages of life. From picture books and graphic novels to fiction and non-fiction, each audience may favour different types of literature.

Reading Beyond English

Australia's multicultural communities are reflected in the range of languages available through public library collections. Beyond English, libraries provide access to literature in languages such as Chinese, Japanese, French, Spanish and Korean.

Chinese-language books recorded the highest checkout numbers among these five languages in both years, while Spanish and Korean were the only languages to record increases.

The butterfly chart compares checkout volumes in 2024 and 2026, revealing how borrowing patterns differ across language collections.

Conclusion

Reading in Australia is not defined by a single generation, community or type of book. Across the country, people engage with reading in different ways, shaped by their interests, circumstances and access to library services. The patterns explored throughout this visualisation reveal important contrasts. Reading participation differs across generations, while library membership, borrowing and visits vary between communities. The books people choose are equally diverse, ranging from fiction and non-fiction to collections serving different audiences and languages.

Yet one connection runs throughout the story: reading is both a personal experience and a shared activity. Libraries provide access to collections and spaces, while recommendations from friends, family and communities help readers discover what to explore next. As physical and digital collections continue to coexist, Australia's reading landscape will keep evolving. Understanding these differences can help libraries and communities respond to changing needs and make reading more accessible. Every reader has a story. And every story begins somewhere.
